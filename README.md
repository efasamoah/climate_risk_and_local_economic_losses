# FUTURE CLIMATE RISK AND ECONOMIC LOSSES IN TROPICAL COASTAL SOCIO-ECOLOGICAL SYSTEMS
Subsistence-oriented communities in tropical coastal areas face the greatest threat from climate change, as their livelihoods depend heavily on climate-sensitive activities such as small-scale fishing and farming. Complementary climate, sustainable development, and biodiversity conservation policies target reducing climate risks, but effective policy outcomes depend on a thorough understanding of system-wide climate risk, community adaptation potential and gaps, and potential economic losses. Using household socioeconomic surveys, climate forecasts, and mapped coastal social-agroecological systems with associated ecosystem service values, we quantified the climate risk and estimated potential economic losses by 2050 for subsistence-oriented coastal communities in 29 sites across four Western Indian Ocean countries. Rising climate risk under low to high emission scenarios (SSP2-4.5-SSP5-8.5) could cause 20–27% annual losses, approximately US$ 106 – US$ 141 million, in five ecosystem benefits by 2050. Weak climate action and adaptation options can widen this adaptation gap. A comprehensive assessment of climate risks and the economic cost of climate inaction can inform policy actions aimed at adapting, mitigating, and compensating for losses caused by climate change.
